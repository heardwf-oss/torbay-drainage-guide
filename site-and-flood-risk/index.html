Evidence for this topic

Documents to look for

  • Site-specific flood risk assessment and dated map extracts
  • Topographical survey, proposed levels, access drawings and any applicable emergency plan
  • Flood-zone compatibility, applicable sequential or exception test evidence, and coastal-risk assessment where relevant

What the evidence should show

  • The site boundary, relevant present and future flood sources, coastal risk where applicable, and the development lifetime.
  • How floor levels, safe access, residual risk and resilience relate to assessed flood conditions.
  • The precise basis for each planning test, exemption, compatibility finding and CDA interpretation.

Critical drainage area 2 checks

FR-01Check the critical drainage area boundaryOfficer guidance

Use the Torbay CDA criteria for conservative screening across Torbay. For a site in Maidencombe, establish the current EA designation and boundary before relying on CDA status in a formal assessment.

Applies when
All development that could affect drainage.
What to check
The site against the current designation map, the applicable local policy and any written confirmation of the 2026 extension. Record the boundary and the basis of the assessment.
Evidence to look for
Dated designation map and recorded assessment basisLocate the application boundary and identify the source version. For Maidencombe, record the published source difference and the confirmation relied on.
Gap to check
A general statement that the whole borough is designated, without addressing the boundary evidence.
Local interpretation

Sources differ. The Local Plan (2015) and SFRA Level 1 (June 2026) say the whole of Torbay is a CDA. The Validation List (November 2024) and the draft SFRA Level 2A (June 2026) say Maidencombe is outside it. The EA national dataset published in September 2026 has a July 2025 geometry revision and does not establish the extension. Whole-Torbay screening is the working interpretation, not confirmation of a new formal boundary. The local Validation List still requires an FRA in Maidencombe.

FR-02Seek a contribution to wider flood risk reductionTorbay criterion

Development in the CDA should be served by SuDS meeting the CDA criteria and should also contribute to a scheme that reduces risk for those liable to flood, for example by improving a constrained channel, culvert or bridge.

Applies when
New development in the CDA, proportionate to scale.
What to check
Whether the proposal identifies or contributes to a downstream improvement. ER1 allows a financial contribution to be requested.
Evidence to look for
Site-specific flood risk assessment and dated map extracts; Topographical survey, proposed levels, access drawings and any applicable emergency plan; Flood-zone compatibility, applicable sequential or exception test evidence, and coastal-risk assessment where relevant
Gap to check
The conclusion cannot be traced to the submitted design or supporting evidence.

Flood risk assessment 4 checks

FR-03Require a flood risk assessment with a surface water drainage strategyRequirement

A flood risk assessment (FRA), including a surface water drainage strategy, is a local validation requirement for householder, full, outline and reserved matters applications and relevant demolition in a conservation area.

Applies when
All listed application types anywhere in Torbay. For prior approvals, compare Validation List 4.1.4 and 4.2.15 and record the applicable planning class, submission scope and assessment basis. Their stated scopes differ; seek Council clarification where applicability is uncertain.
What to check
That an FRA is submitted before validation. Householder development in Flood Zone 1 can normally use the short Torbay proforma.
Evidence to look for
Site-specific flood risk assessment and dated map extracts; Topographical survey, proposed levels, access drawings and any applicable emergency plan; Flood-zone compatibility, applicable sequential or exception test evidence, and coastal-risk assessment where relevant
Gap to check
The conclusion cannot be traced to the submitted design or supporting evidence.

FR-04Require a site-specific FRA where NPPF F4 appliesRequirement

A site-specific FRA should accompany development proposals in Flood Zones 2, 3a and 3b. In Flood Zone 1, it should inform and accompany proposals on sites of 1 ha or more, land the EA has identified as having critical drainage problems, or land identified in an SFRA or the Flood Map for Planning as being at risk from any source now or in future.

Applies when
Any development meeting an F4 trigger. Confirm the current EA-designated boundary where that trigger is material; local FRA requirements also apply.
What to check
The F4 trigger and evidence, and an FRA proportionate to the scale, nature and location of the development, covering relevant flood sources, future climate and lifetime.
Evidence to look for
Site-specific flood risk assessmentIdentify the F4 trigger from the flood zone, site area, EA designation or SFRA/Flood Map evidence. Trace relevant flood sources, future conditions and lifetime through the proportionate assessment.
Gap to check
A single Flood Zone image or an assessment that omits the relevant source or trigger.

NPPF tests 6 checks

FR-06Apply the sequential testConditional

Steer development to the lowest flood risk land where suitable alternative sites are reasonably available. Torbay's Validation List includes a flooding sequential test for new dwellings in Flood Zones 2 and 3. Check the current national exemptions and the applicable local requirements.

Applies when
Development at risk now or in future from any source, unless an exemption in NPPF F5.2 is demonstrated.
What to check
That the sequential test is passed or the precise F5.2 exemption is supported by evidence. The test area should not exceed the anticipated catchment of the development's occupiers or users.
Evidence to look for
Sequential-test evidence or precise exemptionMatch the proposal and FRA to the relevant F5.2 exemption, or demonstrate the sequential test using suitable reasonably available alternatives and the appropriate catchment.
Gap to check
An obsolete change-of-use exemption or an unsupported assertion that the site is exempt.

F5.2 exempts allocated sites tested during plan preparation; sites where an FRA clearly demonstrates that no built development, access or escape routes, land raising or other vulnerable elements are on land at risk now or in future; or surface-water-only risk where layout, design and mitigation meet F7 lifetime safety without increasing risk elsewhere. It also exempts householder development, non-residential extensions with a footprint less than 250 m², and changes of use other than to caravan, camping, chalet, mobile home or park home sites.

FR-07Check flood-zone compatibility and the exception testConditional

Check the use against NPPF Annex F tables 2 and 3: incompatible development should be refused. Where the exception test applies, demonstrate wider sustainability benefits that outweigh flood risk, lifetime safety under F7, and no increase in flood risk elsewhere with an overall reduction where possible.

Applies when
Development in Flood Zones 2, 3a or 3b; the exception-test requirement depends on vulnerability and the exemptions in NPPF F6.2.
What to check
Flood-zone and vulnerability classification, compatibility, all three exception-test criteria supported by the FRA, or the evidenced F6.2 exemption.
Evidence to look for
Flood-zone compatibility and exception-test evidenceCheck Annex F classification and compatibility first, then all three F6.1 criteria or the precise F6.2 exception-test exemption.
Gap to check
A passed exception test used to justify an incompatible use, or a missing lifetime-safety or off-site-risk criterion.

F6.2 removes the exception-test requirement for forms or circumstances exempt from the sequential test under F5, and for allocated sites tested at plan-making stage unless flood risk has since significantly increased or the proposal has changed significantly to introduce a more vulnerable use. This does not remove the separate flood-zone compatibility check.

FR-08Make development safe from floodingRequirement

Development should not present a flood risk to occupiers, users or visitors or increase flood risk elsewhere. Where risk exists now or in future, locate the most vulnerable development in the lowest risk areas unless overriding reasons justify otherwise. Demonstrate lifetime safety for its users, safe management of residual risk and, where appropriate, safe access and escape as part of an agreed emergency plan. Provide flood resistance and resilience allowing rapid return to use without significant refurbishment.

Applies when
All development at risk of flooding now or in future.
What to check
Siting and any overriding reasons, lifetime safety, finished floor levels, residual risk, the applicable emergency plan and access arrangements, resilience and effects elsewhere.
Evidence to look for
Lifetime safety, residual-risk and resilience evidenceRelate the layout, any overriding siting reasons, user vulnerability, applicable emergency plan, access, resilience and effects elsewhere to the assessed present and future flood conditions.
Gap to check
Safe access asserted without the relevant emergency arrangements, or resilience described without an effective return-to-use outcome.

FR-10Coastal change and future tidal inundationConditional

NPPF F9 covers coastal change management areas and other areas likely to face coastal erosion or tidal inundation over the next century. Demonstrate lifetime safety, consistency with the relevant Shoreline Management Plan without unacceptable effects on coastal change processes, and wider sustainability benefits that justify development. Torbay's Validation List requires a coastal change vulnerability assessment within a coastal change management area.

Applies when
Sites within a coastal change management area or other areas within the national F9 risk scope.
What to check
The national coastal-erosion map and current local evidence, all F9 criteria, any applicable local vulnerability-assessment requirement and drainage effects on coastal stability.
Evidence to look for
Coastal-risk scope and assessment evidenceCheck mapped coastal change management areas and the wider national F9 coastal-erosion or tidal-inundation scope. Demonstrate all F9 criteria and the local validation assessment where its trigger applies.
Gap to check
A site outside a mapped coastal change management area treated as automatically outside national F9.
