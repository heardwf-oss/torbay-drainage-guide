Ordinary watercourses

An ordinary watercourse is a stream, ditch, drain or other watercourse that is not designated as a main river. Torbay Council is the consenting authority for ordinary watercourses in its area.

Obstructions, new culverts and alterations to culverts that affect flow require written consent under the Land Drainage Act. Include temporary works in the assessment. Obtain consent before starting; there is no retrospective consent route.

Read Torbay's consent guidance and application route.

Main rivers and flood defences

Check the Environment Agency's main river designation. Works in or near main rivers, flood defences or associated structures can require a flood-risk activity permit.

Check the Environment Agency flood-risk activity permit requirements.

Sewers, highways and land rights

  • Agree sewer connections and receiving capacity with the sewerage undertaker. In Torbay, use South West Water's building and development services.
  • Agree highway drainage, works and adoption arrangements with the highway authority where relevant.
  • Secure landowner permission and lifetime rights for drainage on or across third-party land.
  • Check environmental discharge permits and groundwater protection requirements with the Environment Agency.

Protect existing watercourses

National policy F8 seeks to keep existing open watercourses open unless there are compelling reasons for enclosure. It also supports removing culverts and restoring channels where this can be done without increased flood risk, environmental harm or harm to heritage assets.

Keep access for inspection and maintenance and demonstrate how the watercourse and receiving system will operate.
