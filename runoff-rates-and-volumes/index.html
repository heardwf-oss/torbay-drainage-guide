Documents to look for

  • Greenfield calculation and contributing-area schedule
  • Coordinated hydraulic model and input schedule

What the evidence should show

  • Which national branch applies, using developed volume discharged to surface waters or sewers compared with greenfield runoff for the same 1% AEP, six-hour event.
  • Applicable local limits shown separately, with their geographical and policy basis.
  • Lifetime, climate allowance, urban creep, rainfall and duration assumptions reconciled with the drawings.

Discharge limits 8 checks

RV-01Control rate and volume for the 1% and 50% eventsRequirement

When discharging to a water body or sewer, control the 1% AEP runoff (rate and volume) so flood risk elsewhere does not increase, and the 50% AEP rate so it has no negative impact. Size infiltration for the design event.

Applies when
All positive discharges.
What to check
Event table showing 50%, 3.3% and 1% AEP rates.
Evidence to look for
Greenfield calculation and contributing-area schedule; Coordinated hydraulic model and input schedule; Event results, volume comparison and non-technical summary
Gap to check
The conclusion cannot be traced to the submitted design or supporting evidence.

RV-041% AEP limit: volume testRequirement

Compare the volume discharged to surface waters or sewers in the developed 1% AEP 6-hour event with greenfield runoff volume for the same event. If greater, limit the 1% AEP peak to max(the 50% AEP greenfield rate, 3 l/s/ha). If equal or less, limit it to max(the 1% AEP greenfield rate, 3 l/s/ha). Distinguish runoff generated from the volume remaining to be discharged after interception, reuse or infiltration.

Applies when
Discharge to surface waters or sewers.
What to check
The 6-hour discharged-volume comparison, its contributing area and the national branch used.
Evidence to look for
Greenfield runoff and developed discharged-volume comparisonCompare the same 1% AEP, six-hour event and contributing area. Identify the developed volume discharged to surface waters or sewers after interception, reuse or infiltration, then select the correct national discharge-limit branch.
Gap to check
Peak rates alone, or generated runoff used without identifying the volume discharged to surface waters or sewers.

RV-05Compare the applicable national and local limitsOfficer guidance

Calculate the national 50% AEP limit and the correct 1% AEP volume branch. Separately show the applicable local greenfield performance and Q10 ceiling. Use the more stringent applicable criterion for each event where the evidence-based local approach satisfies national standard 3.10. Do not raise a calculated limit automatically to 1.5 l/s.

Applies when
Positive discharges where national and local criteria overlap.
What to check
Use the runoff comparison; record the local policy basis, the governing event and any agreed departure.
Evidence to look for
National and local event comparisonShow each applicable limit, its event, site scope and policy basis. Record how a source difference or agreement affects the chosen criterion.
Gap to check
One blended discharge limit with no explanation of the national and local tests.
Local interpretation

National 3.10 gives a more stringent, evidence-based local approach precedence. Torbay guide 3.5 says the CDA criteria take preference where they overlap. The CDA's 'mimic greenfield' has no 3 l/s/ha floor, so for small or low-runoff sites it is stricter than national 3.18.

RV-06Check proposals relying on the published 1.5 l/s minimumTorbay criterion

The September 2022 Torbay guide states a minimum positive discharge of 1.5 l/s. Where that exceeds a current national or applicable local limit, record the conflict and obtain the approving body's agreement to the proposed approach.

Applies when
A proposal relies on 1.5 l/s despite a lower calculated limit.
What to check
The lower calculated limit, evidence for the proposed control, blockage protection and the recorded approval or departure.
Evidence to look for
Greenfield calculation and contributing-area schedule; Coordinated hydraulic model and input schedule; Event results, volume comparison and non-technical summary
Gap to check
The conclusion cannot be traced to the submitted design or supporting evidence.
Local interpretation

This published local minimum is not a national exemption. It can conflict with low greenfield rates on sites of different sizes. The comparison tool leaves the conflict visible.

RV-07Brownfield sites: check the greenfield expectationTorbay criterion

Torbay CDA advice and the published local guide expect brownfield runoff to meet greenfield criteria. National standard 3.21 permits an evidenced, agreed relaxation in defined circumstances, capped by 3.21.1. Do not assume that route overrides a more stringent applicable local approach.

Applies when
Previously developed sites.
What to check
Greenfield rates used, not existing rates.
Evidence to look for
Greenfield calculation and contributing-area schedule; Coordinated hydraulic model and input schedule; Event results, volume comparison and non-technical summary
Gap to check
The conclusion cannot be traced to the submitted design or supporting evidence.
Local interpretation

National 3.21 allows a relaxation of up to 5× greenfield with evidence and agreement. The CDA and Torbay guide require brownfield to match greenfield, so assess whether the local approach takes precedence under national 3.10. Record the applicable geographic and policy basis and any requested departure.

Design inputs 3 checks

RV-10Climate change: upper end allowanceRequirement

Use the latest EA allowances, upper end, for the epoch matching the design life. Torbay (South Devon catchment), 2070s epoch (2061 to 2125): +40% for 3.3% AEP and +50% for 1% AEP. 2050s epoch (2022 to 2060): +35% and +45%. Record the development's lifetime and how it is covered by the selected epoch.

Applies when
3.3% and 1% AEP design events.
What to check
Allowance and epoch against the design life.
Evidence to look for
Lifetime and climate input scheduleMatch catchment, development lifetime, epoch and upper-end allowance to the source and hydraulic model inputs.
Gap to check
A percentage copied from an older report without checking the lifetime and current source.
Local interpretation

National SuDS standard 3.8 specifies the upper-end allowance for drainage design. EA general surface-water FRA guidance separately describes central allowances for shorter lifetimes; do not substitute those for the national SuDS design requirement. The Torbay guide's 50% matches the 2070s upper end for the 1% event. For a shorter-lifetime proposal where the current national allowance is lower, record how the published local 50% criterion is applied and obtain agreement for the assessment basis.

RV-11Urban creep: 10% on the proposed impermeable areaTorbay criterion

Add 10% to the impermeable area of the proposed development in the calculations.

Applies when
Applications after 1 October 2022.
What to check
Impermeable area × 1.10 in the model.
Evidence to look for
Greenfield calculation and contributing-area schedule; Coordinated hydraulic model and input schedule; Event results, volume comparison and non-technical summary
Gap to check
The conclusion cannot be traced to the submitted design or supporting evidence.
Local interpretation

National 3.33 applies 10% to impermeable area within property curtilages, and 0% for flats without private permeable space. Torbay's published expectation covers the whole proposed impermeable area. Show both scopes where they differ. Record the model areas, assessment basis and any council agreement; approval of a blanket reconciliation is not recorded.
