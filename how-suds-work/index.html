How SuDS work

Manage rainfall near where it falls, using features that slow, store, treat or reuse it.

What sustainable drainage does

Development changes how rainwater moves. Roofs, roads and paving can increase the amount and speed of runoff. SuDS manage that water using a natural approach, with features distributed through the site.

A system can include rainwater reuse, planted areas, permeable paving, channels, ponds and storage. The right combination depends on the ground, levels, available space and the receiving drainage system.

How the features work together

  1. Manage water near its source. Reuse it or direct it into a suitable local feature.
  2. Slow and treat the flow. Use soil, vegetation and sediment controls as part of a treatment train.
  3. Store larger flows. Keep enough capacity for the design rainfall and release runoff at an acceptable rate.
  4. Provide a safe route for excess water. Protect buildings, people and surrounding land if capacity is exceeded.

The benefits to check

The national standards combine flood-risk control with water quality, amenity, biodiversity and lifetime maintenance. Good design considers these together from the start.

Use drainage requirements for the criteria and SuDS features for the design choices.
