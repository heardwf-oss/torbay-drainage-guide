Drainage glossary

Find plain-language definitions of the terms used in this guide.

Curtilage

Land around a building or group of buildings forming one enclosure for occupants’ private use. It need not equal the application red-line boundary. National glossary · Urban creep check RV-11.

Annual exceedance probability AEP

The chance that a rainfall or flood event of a stated size will be exceeded in any one year. A 1% annual chance is commonly called a 1 in 100 year event.

Amenity

The benefit a place provides to people, such as useful open space, enjoyment and an attractive setting.

Attenuation

Temporary storage that slows the rate at which runoff is released.

Biodiversity

The variety of living things and habitats.

Bioretention

A planted drainage feature using soil or engineered media to store and treat runoff. Rain gardens can be bioretention features.

Brownfield

Previously developed land. Its existing drainage does not automatically set an acceptable new discharge rate.

Catchment

The area from which water drains to a particular point or system.

Combined sewer

A sewer receiving both foul sewage and surface-water runoff.

Critical drainage area CDA

An area identified by the Environment Agency as having critical drainage problems.

Design event

The rainfall event used to check drainage performance, including the specified climate allowance.

Exceedance

Flow occurring when rainfall or other conditions exceed the drainage system's design capacity.

Flood locking

When high water levels at an outfall prevent or reduce drainage discharge.

Freeboard

A vertical margin between a design water level and a level that needs protection.

Greenfield runoff

Estimated runoff from the site in its undeveloped state, used as a design comparison.

Infiltration

Water soaking into the ground.

Interception

Keeping runoff from leaving the site through reuse, infiltration or loss to the atmosphere.

Lead Local Flood Authority LLFA

The authority with local flood-risk responsibilities. Torbay Council is the LLFA for Torbay.

Management train

A linked sequence of drainage features that controls and treats runoff.

Non-potable water

Water used for a purpose that does not require drinking-water quality, such as toilet flushing.

Outfall

The point where a drainage system discharges water.

Qbar and Qmed

Statistical estimates of typical annual flood flow used in runoff assessment. National standard 3.27 allows either as an equivalent 50% annual-chance greenfield rate.

Rainwater harvesting

Collecting and storing rainfall for use.

Runoff

Water that flows over or drains from a surface after rain.

SuDS

Sustainable drainage systems: features that manage surface water using a natural approach and provide several benefits.

Surface-water sewer

A sewer intended to carry surface-water runoff, rather than foul sewage.

Surcharge

When the water level rises above the inside top of the pipe (its soffit), so the pipe flows under pressure. Surcharge does not necessarily mean water reaches the ground surface; surface flooding occurs when water escapes onto the surface. Water UK Appendix C v2.2, C6.2.3 note (printed page 57).

Urban creep

Additional hard surfaces introduced over time, such as paving private gardens.
